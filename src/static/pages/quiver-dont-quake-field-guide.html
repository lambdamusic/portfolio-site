Coda / my take

My take

This was an inspiring read. Most of what I read about AI and creativity is either hype or alarm. Sadek is optimistic without being naive: he gives a whole interlude to why we should quake, and a chapter to his critics. What stays with me is the reframe in the title, the same tremor read as excitement instead of fear, and the practical advice that goes with it: treat AI as a partner in a conversation rather than a vending machine, ask for fifty ideas and have the confidence to discard forty-nine, and defend your why. It made me want to make things, which is more than most books on AI do.

In a world of frictionless creation, the scarce thing is human taste in service of a purpose, and taste is formed by exactly the friction AI removes.

That's my working thesis after reading Sadek. I agree with him that once generating things is cheap, selection and taste are what count. Where I part ways is on where taste comes from. Juniors learn it doing routine work, musicians learn it at the instrument, and those are the parts AI is best at taking away. The value of the Panthropic is real, but the "someone" in the conversation is you.

Sadek gives a whole chapter to his critics and answers most of them. Two get only a general closing: Jaron Lanier on provenance and Cory Doctorow on attention. They're also the two closest to my own work, and where I think there's more to say than the book does.
