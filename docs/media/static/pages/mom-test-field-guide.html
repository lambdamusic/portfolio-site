Avoiding bad data

Three kinds of bad data, and what to do with each

Compliments

  • "That's so cool, I love it!"
  • "Great idea."
Deflect them.

Approval doesn't make the business better. Steer back to their life.

Fluff

  • "I usually…"
  • "I would totally pay for that."
  • "I might use something like this."
Anchor it.

Pin generic and future claims to a real instance: "When did that last happen?"

Opinions, ideas, requests, emotions

  • "You should add X."
  • "That drives me crazy."
Dig beneath them.

"What would that let you do? How are you coping without it? Why haven't you fixed this already?"

The process

Before, during and after every conversation

Before

  1. Choose a focused, findable segment.
  2. With your team, decide the big 3 learning goals.
  3. Decide the ideal next steps and commitments.
  4. Write best guesses about what they care about.
  5. Answer anything you can with desk research first.

During

  1. Frame it and keep it casual.
  2. Ask questions that pass the Mom Test.
  3. Deflect compliments, anchor fluff, dig beneath signals.
  4. Take good notes.
  5. Press for commitment and next steps.

After

  1. Review notes and key quotes with the team.
  2. Move notes into permanent storage.
  3. Update your beliefs and plans.
  4. Decide the next 3 big questions.

Results of a good meeting

Facts, commitment, advancement

Facts

Concrete, specific facts about what they do and why they do it.

Commitment

They show they're serious by giving up something they value.

timereputationcash

Advancement

They move to the next step of your real-world funnel, closer to a sale.

It isn't a real lead until you've given them a concrete chance to reject you. If you don't know what happens next, the meeting was pointless.

Bad finishes

  • "That's so cool. I love it!"
  • "Looks great. Let me know when it launches."
  • "I would definitely buy that."

Good finishes

  • "What are the next steps?"
  • "When can we start the trial?"
  • "Can I buy the prototype?"
  • "When can you come back to talk to the rest of the team?"
